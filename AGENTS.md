## H3 storyboarding & performance -- external skill, use it when writing shot_prompt content

**https://github.com/phileiny/h3-storyboard-skill** (`skills/h3-storyboard/SKILL.md`, MIT, in Traditional Chinese) is a genuinely empirically-derived (not just doc-derived) guide to getting real facial/emotional performance out of H3 -- read the full SKILL.md before writing any `shot_prompt` with an emotional beat, not just this summary. It complements `minimax-h3-skill` (prompt syntax/ComfyUI setup) with the part neither the official nor community skills cover: how to structure a script into shots that H3 can actually perform. Core rules, condensed:

- **Beat density is the central rule (their own words: "don't skip this step").** More than 2-3 expression beats crammed into one shot causes H3 to average them all into a frozen/near-frozen face (measured via PSNR: a single 7s/9-beat shot came back 37-42dB "completely frozen"; the same beats split into three 2-3s/one-beat shots came back 22-23dB "fully rendered"). Count beats before writing prompts; split shots at the 2-3-beat ceiling.
- **Dialogue reallocates the WHOLE clip's frame budget, not just that shot's.** A shot with `<d>` dialogue gets measurably more frames at generation time, at other shots' expense -- don't mix a dialogue shot and a shot that needs background/setting continuity in the same clip, or accept that trade knowingly. Emotion goes in the delivery bracket before the language tag, never inside `<d>` itself (which is verbatim words only).
- **Camera position before expression.** If what a character is looking at isn't in frame, H3 defaults to camera-facing "stage acting." When the character's pose is locked by the story (they must look at X), move the CAMERA to X's direction -- don't add a contradicting pose instruction, it just creates a conflict H3 resolves by picking the story's pose anyway.
- **Physiological, not adjective, emotion descriptions.** Never write "confused"/"shocked"/"irritated" -- describe the actual physical action (their own table: shocked = "head retreats 3cm, upper eyelids fully open, breath held with shoulders staying elevated"). Order matters too: eyebrows first (smallest, earliest) -> eyes -> mouth (last, most consciously controlled).
- **Don't write continuous A-to-B emotional transitions visible in one shot** (e.g. "the crease between her eyebrows smooths out") -- H3 interpolates it into rubbery, unnatural morphing. Hide the transition behind an occlusion (eyes closing, a cut, a hand) instead, reveal the new state only after.
- **Frame-count table**: 124 frames (5.17s) = one static action; 158 (6.58s) = action + one camera move; 192 (8.00s) = entrance/approach; 209 (8.71s) = action->reaction->settle; 243+ (10.13+s) = with cuts. Always leave ~1.3-1.5s of tail buffer -- H3 often degrades to noise in the last 1.2-1.7s, easy to miss in a dark scene at a glance.
- **H3 can't do contact-driven causality or maintain liquid volume.** Don't script the moment of impact/pour -- cut from "about to happen" to "already happened, state now settled."
- **Sizing/distance: describe crop relationships, not fractions or units.** "About two-thirds of frame height" gets ignored (rendered 45-52% in their tests); "ear tip one palm-width from the top edge" doesn't.
- **Don't name objects that shouldn't be in frame** -- H3 tends to hallucinate what it's told about (mentioning "phone screen proportions" can literally generate a phone).
- **The last mile of performance is editing, not more prompt iteration.** H3 treats each timestamp as a state to arrive at cleanly, not a true anticipatory/discontinuous human transition -- if a shot has correct beats/occlusion/counter-moves and still reads as fake, stop reprompting and cut instead (insert-shot to skip a slow reaction; a short insert between two emotional endpoints to remove a too-smooth gradient). Shoot with this in mind: extra length for cutting room, real insert/cutaway shots as edit material, both emotional endpoints fully rendered even if the middle gets discarded.
- **Verify with PSNR, not eyes** (`ffmpeg ... -lavfi "[0:v][1:v]psnr"` on before/after beat frames): >45dB frozen, 30-40dB only breathing-level micro-motion (a failure if a real emotion beat was scripted there), 18-28dB clear motion, <15dB cut-level change.
- **Never delegate to H3**: screen/UI content (always garbled), exact brand text/symbols, pauses and freeze-frames (editing is 100% controllable, generation isn't).

**Applied and validated 2026-08-30** on a 2-shot test scene (Mapache answers a phone call, then reacts with shock) built through this app's own UI via chrome-devtools MCP -- see the video "H3 Storyboard Skill Test - Mapache Phone Call" in this app's own data for the real settings/output. Worth checking that video's actual result before assuming the rules above transfer cleanly to this project's own prompt conventions (cardinality directives, the accent-dubbing block, etc.) -- they were layered on top, not verified in isolation.

## H3 Clip Video Editor (React + FastAPI app, `h3-studio/`)

A small local app for building multi-clip H3 sequences without hand-editing settings JSON: a **video** is an ordered list of **clips**; each clip is one independent MiniMax H3 Ref2VA generation with its own `shot_prompt` (becomes `detailed_description`); the other 5 prompt sections live once on the video as `base_prompt` and are shared by every clip. Regenerating one clip never touches the others; a video's `done` clips can be ffmpeg-concatenated into one file.

- **Backend**: `h3-studio/backend/` (FastAPI). Run from this repo's root, with WanGP's own venv: `E:\repo\wan2.1gp\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m uvicorn backend.main:app --port 8787`. Holds one `shared.api` `WanGPSession` singleton for the process lifetime (created in the FastAPI startup event) — this is what keeps the H3 checkpoint warm across clips instead of reloading it per generation. Job tracking (`jobs.py`) is a direct port of `shared/mcp_server.py`'s `_JobRecord`/`_JobStore` pattern (submit → background watcher thread → poll).
  - **`JobStore` is a real FIFO queue, not a 409-on-busy gate.** Originally `submit()` raised `Busy` (→ HTTP 409) if a generation was already in progress, forcing the caller to retry. Replaced with an internal `collections.deque`: `submit()` always accepts and returns a `job_id` immediately, appending to the queue; if the session is idle it starts right away, otherwise it waits and `_maybe_start_next()` (called both on `submit()` and from the previous job's completion callback) picks it up automatically the moment the active job finishes — no polling, no manual retry, verified with two back-to-back character reference-video generations (first went `running` immediately, second sat `queued` and auto-transitioned to `running` the instant the first hit `done`, zero manual intervention). Added a real `queued`→`running`→`done`/`failed` status lifecycle in the process (previously "running" was never actually set — everything in flight just showed `queued`); the frontend's `badge-running`/`ACTIVE_STATUSES` CSS/logic already anticipated this and needed no changes.
  - **Careful**: since regenerate silently overwrites `output_path` in place, re-triggering `generate` on a character/clip that already has a good final result (e.g. just to test something unrelated, like the queue) will clobber it with whatever cheap/test settings are currently active — happened for real testing the queue above, recovered because one of the two characters happened to have a manual backup (`cp` to `..._720p_backup.mp4`) and the other had to be fully regenerated. **Use a disposable test video/character for infra tests, don't reuse one with a real final output you want to keep**, or back it up first.
  - **Don't use `--reload`; restart manually after editing backend code.** Tried `--reload` after the folder-per-video refactor: it logged `StatReload detected changes... Reloading...` but never actually respawned a new worker (no follow-up `Started server process [pid]` line), so the OLD code kept serving silently, AND it left behind a churn of stale/zombie worker processes each still holding (or appearing to hold) port 8787. Compounding this: **`Get-CimInstance`/`taskkill` by PID can silently fail to find a process that's still very much alive and listening** — `Get-NetTCPConnection -LocalPort 8787 -State Listen | Select OwningProcess` disagreed with `Get-Process -Id <that pid>` more than once in the same debugging session. The reliable kill sequence: `netstat -ano | grep ":8787" | grep LISTENING` for the true owning PID, then `powershell -Command "Stop-Process -Id <pid> -Force"` (not git-bash `taskkill`, which uses a different PID resolution path here and can report "process not found" for a PID that `Get-NetTCPConnection` just reported as the listener). Verify with another `netstat` pass before relaunching, not just trusting the kill command's exit status.
  - **Storage: one folder per video**, not a single shared file. `h3-studio/backend/data/videos/<slug>-<id8>/` (folder name = slugified title + first 8 chars of the video's uuid, computed once at creation and stored as `video["folder"]` so renaming the title later doesn't move anything) holds `config.json` (the full video record: title, template_settings, base_prompt, clips list) and a `clips/<clip_id>.mp4` per generated clip, plus `concat.mp4`/`concat_list.txt` when joined. `store.py`'s `_read_all()`/`mutate()` scan `data/videos/*/config.json` and rewrite every video's own file on each mutation (cheap at this scale) — gitignored as a whole directory (`h3-studio/backend/data/videos/`). `store.create_video()` creates the folder; `store.remove_video()` `shutil.rmtree`s it.
  - **Generated files still land in a flat staging dir first.** `shared.api.init(output_dir=...)` is fixed once per session and can't vary per task, so WanGP always writes a finished generation into `h3-studio/backend/outputs/` (gitignored, transient scratch space only) — the job watcher (`jobs.py::_JobRecord._finalize`) then `shutil.move`s it into the owning video's `clips/<clip_id>.mp4`. Don't treat `outputs/` as a source of truth for any clip's location.
  - Served to the frontend via a `/media` static mount pointed at `data/videos/` (not `/outputs` — that name is stale from before this refactor); `to_output_url()` computes the URL as a path relative to `store.VIDEOS_ROOT`.

  - **The real limitation, found empirically building a 9-clip chained scene**: 3-character continuation chains become unreliable past a certain point, and the failure is **silent** — it doesn't error, it silently clones or blends a character's identity in the new footage, and this corruption then propagates forward, since every subsequent continuation faithfully continues from the now-corrupted tail. **Root-caused by checking a clip's actual LAST 1-2 seconds specifically, not just a mid-shot sample** — a clip can look completely correct (right headcount, right identities) through 90%+ of its own new footage and only drift into a clone/blend in the closing frames, and since those closing frames are exactly what becomes the *next* clip's continuity context, the corruption is invisible until you specifically check the tail.   - **Practical mitigation, not yet automated**: when using continuation chains with 3+ named subjects, check the literal final ~1-2 seconds of every clip (not a mid-shot sample) before trusting it as a continuation source — `ffmpeg -y -ss <duration-2> -i clip.mp4 -vf fps=2 out_%03d.jpg` — since that's the only part of the clip that actually matters for what comes next. If a clip's tail is corrupted, regenerating that SAME clip (same prompt, same source) sometimes reproduces success on retry (clip6→7 succeeded fully on the 2nd content-identical-content attempt once its predecessor was itself fixed) — the corruption isn't deterministic per prompt, so blind retries of the same clip are a legitimate (if unreliable) recovery lever, not just re-rolling prompt wording.
  - **Real cost consequence discovered**: since corruption propagates forward through the whole rest of the chain, catching it late (e.g. only at the very end) means regenerating not just the corrupted clip but every clip generated from it onward — in this session's case, one bad clip 7 required deleting clips 7/8/9 entirely (clips 8 and 9 had been generated — some more than once — continuing from the already-corrupted clip 7, wasting real GPU time on unusable output) rather than a single cheap fix. Checking tails early, right after each clip finishes, is meaningfully cheaper than discovering the corruption several clips later.
  - **`clip.own_segment_path` (2026-08-19)**: every clip's raw output file is the *whole* accumulated chain up to that point (per the additive-length behavior above), which makes reviewing just what changed in one clip tedious — you have to scrub past everything before it every time. `main.py::_compute_own_segment_path`, called from `generate_clip`'s `on_done` right after a continuation clip finishes, `ffprobe`s the *previous* clip's own duration and `ffmpeg -ss <that> -c copy`-trims the new clip's output to just its own new portion, stored as a separate derived file (`{clip_id}_own_segment.mp4`) — the original full-range file is untouched, since continuation/bridging/concat all still need the full range. `clip_view` exposes this as `own_segment_url` (falls back to the regular `output_url` for non-continuation clips, where the whole file already *is* its own segment).
- **Video continuation now supports "bridge to an existing next clip," not just open-ended extension (2026-08-19).** H3's end-frame conditioning (`image_prompt_type` `"E"` + `image_end`, confirmed from `wgp.py`: the `image_end` field, and `end_frames_always_enabled(model_def)` returning `True` for H3 so `"E"` combines freely with `"V"` rather than needing `"S"`/`"L"` too) lets a generation be steered to land on a specific target frame. Wired in as `clip.bridge_to_next` (bool) — when set, `main.py::generate_clip` extracts the *next* clip's own first frame via `ffmpeg -frames:v 1` (400s if that next clip isn't `done` yet) and passes it to `prompt.py::build_generation_settings(..., bridge_end_frame_path=...)`, which sets `image_prompt_type += "E"` + `image_end = [path]` alongside the existing `"V"`/`video_source` continuation wiring — the two combine into `"VE"`. **This is the real fix for "editing a middle clip stales the whole rest of the chain"**: regenerate clip N with `bridge_to_next` pointed at clip N+1, and clip N+1 (and everything after it) never needs to be touched, since the new clip N is steered to reconnect almost exactly where the old one did. **Confirmed via pixel-diff**: the regenerated clip's actual last frame vs. the target frame extracted from the untouched next clip came out to a mean abs diff of ~4.5/255 — essentially the same frame, well inside re-encoding noise, not a fresh independent render that happens to look similar.
- **`POST /videos/{id}/concat` had a real correctness bug, found and fixed 2026-08-19**: it concatenated every `status=="done"` clip's *raw* output file unconditionally. Since a continuation clip's file already contains everything before it, this meant joining, say, 6 chained clips would replay clip 1's content six times over (each subsequent clip's full accumulated file, one after another) — a video whose total duration is the *sum* of all six already-cumulative durations instead of just the last one. **Fixed** by walking clips in order and skipping any clip whose immediate successor has `continue_from_previous` set (that successor's file already fully contains it) — only the last clip of each continuous run gets included. Handles mixed independent/continuation topologies correctly too (verified by reasoning through a case with two separate 2-clip continuation runs). For a chain that's continuous end-to-end (this app's normal case so far), the "join" is now just the last clip's own file, unchanged — concat's real job is stitching together multiple *independent* (non-continuation) segments.

- **Scene Continuity & "Scene Memory" Across Cuts (Explored, Tested & Documented 2026-08-31)**:
  - **The 3 Continuity Paradigms & Their Exact Empirical Behaviors**:
    1. **Paradigm 1: Independent Clips with Static Frame Reference (`<Picture 3>`)**:
       - *Result*: **~80–85% consistency**. The office set, desk props, and actors are recognizable, but head tilt, initial eye-gaze, and micro-lighting twitch slightly at the cut point because each clip starts at Frame 0 from pure random Gaussian noise.
    2. **Paradigm 2: In-Prompt Internal Camera Cuts (`CAMERA A ... Cut to CAMERA B`)**:

With a locked static camera (`"Static camera at eye-level, strictly NEVER cuts, pans, zooms, or changes angle"`), background furniture, wall props, lighting, and actors remain 100% stable without any shifts across 30+ seconds (tested & validated in `FBI Homicide - Plano Secuencia Continuo (720p)`).

  - **Luma Attenuation / Darkening Drift in Continuation Chains (Identified & Verified 2026-08-31)**:
    - *Symptom*: In iterative 5s continuation chains (`continue_from_previous: true`), subsequent clips become progressively darker.
    - *Measurement*: Measured across 6 chained segments:
      - Clip #0 (0–5s): Mean luminance $Y = 33.33 / 255$
      - Clip #1 (5–10s): $Y = 29.73 / 255$
      - Clip #2 (10–15s): $Y = 26.40 / 255$
      - Clip #3 (15–20s): $Y = 23.96 / 255$
      - Clip #4 (20–25s): $Y = 21.52 / 255$
      - Clip #5 (25–30s): $Y = 18.73 / 255$ (a cumulative **44% drop** in brightness).

- **Multi-Male Voice Bleed & Distinct Voice Conditioning (Explored & Solved 2026-08-31 / 2026-09-01)**:
  - *Problem*: When 2 male characters (e.g. Santamaría + Markos or Mapache + Markos) are referenced together in MiniMax H3 Ref2VA, the audio cross-attention layers tend to bleed their vocal timbres toward an average mid-range male voice if prompt directives lack explicit contrast.
  - *Fix*:
    1. **Strict Audio Reference Citations in Dialogue**: Every `<d>` tag must explicitly include `voice matches <Audio N> exactly` (e.g., `<Subject 1> (S1) <d>[Speaker S1, voice matches <Audio 1> exactly, high-energy anxious tenor voice, fast delivery, Neutral Latin American Spanish, use tú] ...</d>`).
    2. **Extreme Pitch & Timbre Separation**: Pair a high/mid tenor voice (Mapache / Santamaría) with a deep low-bass baritone voice (Markos).
    3. **Audio Duration Ceiling Compliance**: Keep each character's reference audio at ~6.0s so their combined total remains strictly $\le 15.0\text{s}$ (MiniMax H3 hard limit).
